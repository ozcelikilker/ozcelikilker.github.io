## Course Overview

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to the architecture and protocols of the Internet using a top-down approach, beginning with the Application Layer to provide immediate context through familiar services like HTTP, DNS, and video streaming. 

Throughout the semester, we will cover Chapters 1 through 6:
* **Chapter 1: Computer Networks and the Internet** – Introduction to the network edge, core, delays, protocol layers, and security.
* **Chapter 2: Application Layer** – Web/HTTP, FTP, electronic mail, DNS, P2P applications, and socket programming.
* **Chapter 3: Transport Layer** – Multiplexing/demultiplexing, UDP, TCP congestion control, and connection-oriented transport.
* **Chapter 4: The Network Layer (Data Plane)** – Forwarding and routing functions, routers, IP, and SDN (Software-Defined Networking).
* **Chapter 5: The Network Layer (Control Plane)** – Routing algorithms (LS, DV), OSPF, BGP, and SDN control planes.
* **Chapter 6: The Link Layer and LANs** – Error detection/correction, multiple access links, Switched LANs, and link virtualization.

As we descend the protocol stack, we explore the transport layer's mechanisms for reliability and congestion control (TCP/UDP), the dual nature of the network layer - covering both the hardware-driven data plane and the algorithmic control plane - and finally, the link layer’s role in local area networks and data centers. Students will understand how applications exchange data to mastering the complex routing, switching, and physical transmission principles that sustain global connectivity.

## Learning Outcomes

- The students learn principles of computer networks.
- The students establish and review computer networks.
- The students can generate Network-capable software that communicates over the Internet.
